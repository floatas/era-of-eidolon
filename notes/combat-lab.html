Skeleton

    The hit test and the step can live on the server. A creature already walks and swings in the playback, so a skeleton can be made to miss unless you stand on the one cell they face, and they can spend their step turning to put that cell on the square you occupied when the turn opened.

    The red squares cannot. During a fight the phone paints a square only for the path you queued and the cell you are aiming at. It has no message for “these cells belong to the skeleton.” Painting their pattern means changing the 2005 drawing code. Until that exists, the pattern still changes the fight: the swing misses, and one line can say which way they face.

    They plan blind, the same way they do now. The amber square is where they will try to stand, aimed at where you are before you move. Stepping off that cell is the dodge. On a tie, your step is already played first.

    A second phase fits inside the fight the phone already has. At zero life we do not remove her and we do not tell the quest she died. The playback says she drops the bow, a heal fills the bar, and the next swings use the other plan. The quest hears about the death only when the second pool is gone.

    Her picture stays. A node is tied to one creature sheet for the whole fight, and Mira is already drawn on the people sheet. The change is the weapon and the cells she threatens, said out loud, not a new sprite.

    Shirard is the same machine. Both of them finish in melee with the same five cells: the rank in front and both flanks. The pocket is behind them. Standing on a shoulder, which was safe against a golem, is a hit.

    This is one walk, in the order the doors suggest, counting each creature once. Wildlife comes back later, so a patient player can earn more than this. Quest enemies do not.

    A kill pays its life in gold, and the same amount in experience (at least 8). A level costs 65, then 5 more each level, and each level is 2 raises and 6 more life. The five traders already show a sliding shelf. Lava Island starts at 32 gold. The Core opens near 850.

    The blow is not the life total. Most creatures carry their own hit number, and it climbs on its own. The Old Shrine skeletons have 10 life and a blow of 19. Shirard and Mira have 10 life and a blow of 8, because their rows never got that number and fall back to a formula. The life curve replaces both, and then a blow is 6 plus a quarter of the new life.

    Crates are extra gold on top of this sheet. Kills only, and the purse is unspent, so the shop column is “could you afford it.” A requirement is checked as if every raise went into the one stat that item asks for.
