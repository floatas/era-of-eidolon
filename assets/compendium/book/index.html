Shirard's Forge Companion

Everything the game does not tell you, in the order you need it: your hero, how a fight works, the three kinds of weapon, the shops, and then the route, room by room, with each creature where you first meet it.

Your hero

You start at level 0 with 40 life, 0 gold and every ability at 3 (Strength 3, Intelligence 3, Dexterity 3, Toughness 3, Willpower 3, Speed 3). You carry the Bloody Blade and wear the Scrapsuit; the Short Bow is in your pack for when you want it.

Levels

A level costs 60, 80, 100, 120… experience, twenty more each time. A kill pays by the creature and its tier; a boss pays once, when it finally falls. Each level gives 6 more life (plus whatever Toughness adds), a full heal and 2 raise points. One point adds 3 to an ability under 48, 2 under 78 and 1 above that. A full clear of the chapter lands you near level 12.

Where to put the third point

Put two of every three points into your weapon's ability: Strength for a blade, Dexterity for a bow. The third point is a choice of style, and all three work:

  • Dexterity (Strength for an archer): you hit and dodge more often, or wear better suits. The all-rounder.
  • Toughness: the tank. About twice the life by the end of the chapter and a third more mistakes survived, at the cost of fights running a little longer.
  • Speed: the tempo fighter. Fights end sooner and bosses fall faster, and a sword's shorter swing fits between more enemy blows. Fewer mistakes are survivable.

The fight

A turn is 720 units. You plan yours, then both sides play out on the same clock: your blows and theirs land in time order. Walking into place usually takes a few steps first.

Hitting and being hit

Your blow lands 100 + half your Dexterity − the creature's avoidance (about 12 to 16) times in a hundred. Half of the blows that land are precise (×1.5) and one in fifteen is a critical (×2). A creature's blow lands 70 − your avoidance (half your Dexterity, plus Dodge). Your suit takes its E off every blow that lands.

Shapes

No creature strikes every square around it. Each one strikes its own shape, shown on its card, drawn facing right. It picks its facing before the turn plays and walks until its shape covers the square you started on. Step off that shape before its blow lands and the blow misses: Out of reach. One whole turn standing inside a shape costs about a third of the life you are expected to have in that tier. Hold a bow and every ordinary creature instead walks up and strikes all eight squares around it. Bosses keep their shapes.

Practice

Every action and every weapon line has a title: Fumbler, Beginner, Trained, Experienced, Master, Expert, Sensei. Each use that lands adds 5% towards the next one, kept only if you win the fight. Each title adds 1 to that action's effect and takes 5% off its time.

How your ability adds

A blow adds a third of your Strength (Dexterity for a bow), scaled by how long the swing takes against 120. At Strength 35 the Ghost Sword (Ti126) adds 12 to each of its 4 blows a turn, about 48; the Doom Hammer (Ti240) adds 23 to each of its 2, about 46. Raising your ability helps every weapon about equally, so pick the weapon for the job and the ability for the weapon.

The footwork rule

A blow is safe from its target when stepping in, swinging and stepping out (80 + the swing) is shorter than the time between two of its blows. Each creature's card says which blades manage it.

Items and shops

Reading a market line

Corrupter E-2 Ti96 240g Giant Sword E9 Ti120 460g Snail Shell E4 100g
  • E on a weapon is added to every blow; it can be negative on a cheap weapon. On a suit it is taken off every blow you take.
  • Ti is how much of the 720-unit turn one swing takes. Suits have none.
  • The price comes last. What a piece asks for is not on the line: buying one you cannot wear yet tells you, as in You need Strength 13.

The colours

  • Green: better than what you wear in that slot, and you can pay for it.
  • Red: worse than what you wear.
  • Grey: your ability is still too low to wear it.
  • No colour: a different kind of weapon from yours, or better but beyond your purse.

A weapon is compared by the damage it deals over a turn, not by its E, so a fast sword with a small E can be green next to a hammer with a big one.

Selling

Traders do not buy. Recycle on an item breaks it and gives you 30% of its price. It is the only way to turn gear back into gold, and every crate holds something worth keeping or breaking.

Traders and crates

Five traders, one plan per tier. Each trader sells its own tier; where a tier has two, the nearer one sells the cheaper half and the further one the better half, and each keeps the best piece of the shop before it. Every crate holds an item. A few on the main path hold the next step up for you: a better weapon of the kind in your hand, or a better suit. Every other crate holds a spare from that tier or below that you do not carry yet: switch to it, or recycle it for gold. Past tier I no crate holds the best piece of its tier, which is the trader's. The first crate in the Village gives a new hero a better weapon he can wear at once.

Charms, bombs and the other skills and spells in the catalogue do nothing in this chapter and are not sold.

The route

Rooms in the order the chapter is meant to be walked. A creature's card appears where you first meet it. On each card, red squares are where it strikes, green are safe squares beside it, and the pale circle is the creature itself, facing right.
